How it works

      
One conversation. Then it gets on with it.

      
Most of a launch week goes on copying the same tagline into 400 slightly different forms. launchkit gives that part to your agent and keeps the decisions with you.

    

    

      
Interview
It reads your site and repo first, then asks only what it couldn't find, about five questions at a time.

      
Knowledge base
Your answers go into one profile, along with copy at every length forms ask for: 40, 60, 160, 300 and 1000 characters.

      
Submit
It claims a site, reads that site's guide, fills in the form in the browser and records the result. One worker, or several at once.

      
Report
You get a list of what's live and what's pending, plus a short to-do list: CAPTCHAs, email checks and drafts to approve.

The interview

        
It asks like a co-founder, not a form.

        
It researches before it asks anything. You confirm what it found, fill the gaps and pick your rules: budget, how many workers, and whether it can create accounts.

      

      

        
I read acme.dev. Is the tagline "Turn Figma files into clean React code", aimed at frontend teams of 2 to 20?

        
Yes, but say "production React", not "clean".

        
Got it. I found your logo in /public. Do you have 3 to 5 screenshots, or should I take them from the live site at 1440px?

        
Grab them from the site.

        
Last one: budget for paid listings? At $0 I'll skip the 38 paid-only sites and choose free tiers everywhere else.

        
$0. Run 2 workers, I'll be around for CAPTCHAs.

      

    

  


  

    

      
Site guides

      
Every directory gets its own playbook.

      
32 research agents opened every site's live submission flow and wrote down what they found: the fields, the limits, the login, the paid upsells to avoid and what gets a listing featured. Your agent reads the guide before it touches the form.

Make the listing perform

        
Days reset at 12:01 AM Pacific, so launch then to get the full 24 hours. Tuesday to Thursday has the most traffic and the most competition.

        
Real gallery images and an active maker get featured more often than raw upvotes do.

        
Don't ask for upvotes or share direct vote links. Product Hunt penalises it.

Human writing

        
No AI slop. No em dashes.

        
Directory editors and Reddit mods can spot machine-written copy in a sentence. A writing skill and a linter keep yours plain and specific, and true to your profile.

        
$ launchkit lint-copy --profile

Safety rails

      
Fast, but never reckless with your name.

    

    

      
$
Never pays
It stops at any checkout. Paid-only sites are skipped unless you set a budget, and you complete the payment yourself.

      
?
Never solves CAPTCHAs
If you're nearby, it hands the browser to you. If you're away, the site goes on your to-do list.

      
✎
Never posts unapproved
Reddit, HN and press pitches are drafts until you approve that exact text.

      
*
Never stores passwords
It uses your browser's existing sessions, Google or GitHub sign-in, and your password manager.

      
!
Never invents facts
Every claim comes from your profile. If there's no number, it doesn't make one up.

      
#
One listing per site
A file-locked queue means parallel workers never submit the same site twice.
