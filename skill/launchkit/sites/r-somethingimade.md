# r/somethingimade

About 3.1 million members, but it's for **handmade physical crafts only**. The rules explicitly ban apps and websites, digital items, commercial products, and business accounts. Software startups don't fit.

## Before you start
- Unless the product is a physical item the founder made by hand (no 3D printing, no CNC, no team), mark `skipped` with "handmade crafts only, apps banned".
- If it does fit: one item per post, a photo, and a description of what it is, what it's made of, and how it was made.

## Field map (only for handmade physical products)

| Field on the site | Limit / format | Fill with |
|---|---|---|
| Community | picker | r/somethingimade |
| Post type | Image (no video, no links) | One photo of the item (not a gallery of several items) |
| Title | 300 chars | What the item is, e.g. "Walnut desk organiser I made for my partner" |
| Body / caption | text | What it is, materials, the process. No store link |
| Flair | mod-assigned | Users can't pick flair |

## Steps
1. Confirm the product is a handmade item. If not, `launchkit mark r-somethingimade skipped --note "handmade crafts only"`.
2. Otherwise draft the post with `launchkit draft r-somethingimade -` and mark `drafted`. Post only with user approval and `allow_social_posting`.

## Make the listing perform
- Progress photos and a clear process write-up do well. A store link is only allowed in the user's profile, and only if the store is a side project.

## Gotchas
- Limit of 2 posts per user per week. The same item can't be reposted within 6 months.
- No AI-written posts. Professional-looking stores lead to a ban.
