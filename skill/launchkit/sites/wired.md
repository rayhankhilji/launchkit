# Wired

Condé Nast's science and technology magazine. The pitch page is written for freelance writers (narrative features, about $1 per word), not for companies, and Wired rarely covers small product launches. Realistic routes for a founder: a news story the business desk would care about, or a product for the Reviews team (consumer gear and software).

## Before you start
- Be honest with the user: odds are very low unless there is a larger story (a trend the product is part of, unusual data, a policy fight).
- Reviews: the FAQ says the team accepts review samples from brands but promises nothing. Only relevant for consumer hardware or apps.
- No form. Email only.

## Field map

| Field | Limit / format | Fill with |
|---|---|---|
| To | email | the section editor listed on the pitch page; for startups and companies that's the senior editor, business (listed with email and Signal at last check) |
| Subject | under 80 chars | the story, not the product |
| Body | under 150 words | the larger change the product shows, the evidence (data, customers, documents), and why now; `product.name` and `product.url` as supporting detail |
| Sign-off | text | `founder.name`, `founder.role`, `founder.email` |

## Steps
1. Open https://www.wired.com/about/how-to-pitch-stories-to-wired/ and pick the editor whose section matches (business, science, security, gear).
2. Write a story pitch, not a launch announcement. `lk draft wired -`, mark `drafted`.
3. Send only with user approval. Never use the Signal handles for product pitches.

## Make the listing perform
- The page says: "pitch us a tale you're going to tell, not a topic". Give a concrete scene or finding.
- Wired looks for "faint signals of change". Frame the product as evidence of a shift, with numbers from the profile.
- For gear and app reviews, offer a review unit or account and a clear embargo date.

## Gotchas
- press@wired.com is for press inquiries about Wired itself, and mail@wired.com is letters to the editor. Neither is for pitches.
- Wired doesn't publish AI-generated text. Keep the pitch plainly human (skill/human-writing).

## After submitting
- No auto-reply. Don't follow up more than once.
